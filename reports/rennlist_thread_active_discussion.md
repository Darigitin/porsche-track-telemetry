718 Cayman GT4 Telemetry Debrief: 1:56.55 PB at High Plains Raceway Hey everyone, I wrapped up my 2026 track season this past weekend at High Plains Raceway (Full Course - 2.55 Miles) and wanted to share a comprehensive telemetry and setup debrief. My car is a 2023 718 Cayman GT4 in Guards Red running Apex VS-5RE forged wheels (19x9 front / 19x11 rear) wrapped in Vitour Tempesta P1 (P-01R) tires (265/35R19 front / 295/30R19 rear). When I first brought the car out in June 2024, my baseline was a humble 2:16.78 . Over the course of 61 sessions and 519 timed CAN telemetry laps , I systematically chipped away at my inputs, tire pressures, and braking technique down to an official 1:56.55 . The Tire Pressure Sweet Spot on Vitour Tempesta P1 (~30.5 PSI Hot Target) � Cold Start: 23.5 - 24.0 PSI cold. � The Trap: Starting above 28-30 PSI cold pushed the P1s past 36-38 PSI hot within a few laps, causing the compound to overheat and introducing sharp mid-corner understeer. � The Discipline: Coming in immediately after Session 1 and bleeding down to 30.5 PSI hot front / 31.0 PSI hot rear kept the tire right in its prime grip window�delivering 1.35G sustained and peaking at 1.54G through the Prairie Corkscrew (Turns 13-15). 2. The Turn 4 Revelation (Braking in 3rd vs 4th Gear: Gaining 0.300s) � I compared my July telemetry against my October telemetry through Turn 4 (Biker's Berm) and Turn 5 (Niagara Plunge). � In July, I held 4th gear through the T4 braking zone. In October, I executed an immediate downshift to 3rd gear during threshold braking at 1,364m. � Data Result: - Apex RPM jumped from 4,477 RPM (4th gear) to 5,411 RPM (3rd gear) . - Corner exit acceleration climbed from +0.20G to +0.31G . - Uphill entry speed into T5 climbed from 79.1 MPH to 84.7 MPH . - Total time saved across T4-T5: Exactly 0.300 seconds purely from gear selection and engine braking stability. 3. Trail-Braking Front Axle Loading � The 718 GT4 mid-engine chassis rotates beautifully if I keep 10-15% trail-braking pressure on the nose all the way down to the apex curb. � Bleeding off the brake pedal smoothly rather than snapping off eliminated my mid-corner push in Turns 8 and 10 completely. ____________________________________________________ &#128172; Questions for fellow 718 GT4 track drivers: 1. For those running Tarett adjustable drop links: what sway bar hole settings (soft/med/stiff) are you running to balance turn-in rotation vs high-speed stability? 3. What alignment specs (camber/toe) have given you the best tire life without killing high-speed braking stability? ### Post #20775903 by `GentianGentleman`

Wow this is some great data analysis and you have used it perfectly to whittle down your track times. Bravo! I am hesitant to give you any suggestions because you clearly have all the data already. If you are not using a pyrometer already, you should get one to measure the inside, middle and outside of each tire as soon as you come back into the pits after a session. Recruit a second person to take these actions as soon as you roll into the pits to get the most accurate temps and note them down. feed that data to the ai and this will help you get closer to that ideal alignment setting.

### Post #20776703 by `BoxKing`

Nice improvements! Targeting 30PSI Front/31PSI Rear in RE71-RZ 265/305/19's I have Tarett drop links, Swift 60NM front springs and Porsche Motorsports Style Caster Pucks. I run my front sways M/S, rear at M/M. Im running -3.3F/-2.8R, 0 Toe Front, 5MM total toe in the Rear, Caster ~9. After 10 days on track and over 40 heat cycles, my RE71's looked like they still had half the tread life left on them, extremely even treadwear on all 4 corners. I would have kept using them if I didnt have a 3 day event that was 3.5 hours away (I currently drive the GT4 to the track). I'm an oddball who enjoyed the diminished grip from heat cycling out the tires, I found it more challenging and easier to find the limits of grip &quot;safely&quot;, and I was still achieving new PB's on the last day I drove on them. I'm not harsh on tires, though I still turn solid laps. Driving style predicates tire wear more than car setup. You Tube
